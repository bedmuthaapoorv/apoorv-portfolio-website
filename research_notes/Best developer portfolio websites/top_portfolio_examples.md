# Top Developer Portfolio Websites (2024-2026): Example Catalogue and Patterns

Research date: 2026-11-10. "Visited" means the live site was fetched during this research (text-mode fetch, so visual and motion details are mostly not observed). Anything marked "not visited" is secondhand.

## Which developer portfolios are most cited/admired, and what does each do well?

### Takeaway
The most-cited developer sites fall into three groups. (1) Template-defining "resume as website" sites (Brittany Chiang). (2) Craft and interaction-led design-engineer sites (Rauno Freiberg, Paco Coursey, Emil Kowalski, Bruno Simon, plus Awwwards winners like Henri Heymans). (3) Writing-led sites where the blog is the portfolio (Josh Comeau, Lee Robinson, Julia Evans, Simon Willison, Dan Abramov, Sam Rose, Anthony Fu). The GitHub awesome-list has 27.6k stars and about 2,100 entries. Brittany Chiang's v4 is probably the most-forked developer portfolio design (8.3k stars, 4.2k forks on GitHub).

## What recurring patterns separate the top tier from average portfolios, and what weaknesses do even top sites have?

### Takeaway
Top sites lead with one strong positioning line and then prove it with shipped, named artifacts, quantified outcomes or deep writing. They show an obvious point of view and personality. Average portfolios list skills and tutorial projects. Even top sites often lack dates, case-study depth, and accessibility or markup polish (award sites especially).

### Cited Findings
- **One-line positioning**: Brittany ("I build accessible, pixel-perfect experiences for the web"), Paco ("crafting interfaces"), Anthony Fu ("fanatical open sourceror"), Sam Rose ("visual, interactive essays about software"). — [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com); [paco.me](https://paco.me); [antfu.me](https://antfu.me); [samwho.dev](https://samwho.dev)
- **Proof via named, adopted artifacts**: cmdk and next-themes (Paco), Sonner and Vaul (Emil), Vitest, VueUse and UnoCSS (Fu), "100k+ Installs" for Halcyon theme (Brittany). — [paco.me](https://paco.me); [emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski); [antfu.me](https://antfu.me); [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)
- **Ownership verbs over task lists**: Delba writes "built, authored, maintained" Next.js Docs and describes impact ("used by GTM teams and Customer Support"). — [delba.dev](https://delba.dev)
- **Writing as the moat**: Josh Comeau (interactive tutorials and courses), Julia Evans (60+ categories, zines), Simon Willison (near-daily since 2002), Lee Robinson (notes on beliefs, DX, product engineering). — [joshwcomeau.com](https://www.joshwcomeau.com); [jvns.ca](https://jvns.ca); [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net); [leerob.com](https://leerob.com)
- **Personality and small delights**: the TARDIS easter egg (Brittany), sound toggle (Josh), manifesto (Rauno), playlists and "Now" section (Paco), Whispers board and achievements (Bruno), photos and generative art (Fu). — sites above
- **Version history as a credential**: Rauno's yearly archives (2022, 2023) and Brittany's "time travel" plus public v4 repo show growth over time. — [rauno.me](https://rauno.me); [GitHub bchiang7/v4](https://github.com/bchiang7/v4)
- **Build in public**: Bruno open-sources folio-2025 and posts devlogs. Brittany credits her stack in the footer. — [bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com); [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)
- **Common mistakes named by roundups**: too much information, ignoring mobile, copying trends without a personal touch. Advice: "show, not tell". Projects need context: what, why, results. — [DEV.to](https://dev.to/jtrevdev/best-developer-portfolio-websites-and-why-they-work-3bdk); [WebSearch summary of SiteBuilderReport / Rockstar Developer University](https://rockstardeveloperuniversity.com/web-developer-portfolio-examples/)
- **Weakness, award sites**: Henri Heymans' Awwwards Developer Award scored 8.00 for animations but only 6.60 for accessibility and markup. Spectacle tends to trade off against fundamentals. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/henri-heymans-25)
- **Weakness, staleness and missing dates**: Rauno, Paco, Emil and samwho show no post dates on the homepage. Rauno's newest archive is 2023. Josh's homepage has no dates. Visitors can't tell how current the site is. — [rauno.me](https://rauno.me); [paco.me](https://paco.me); [emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski); [samwho.dev](https://samwho.dev)
- **Weakness, link rot and abandonment**: Cassie Evans replaced her site with a farewell note. The emmabostian list needs link-checker tooling, and some entries still point to old domains (e.g., leerob.io). Bruno's leaderboard was offline. — [cassie.codes](https://www.cassie.codes); [GitHub list](https://github.com/emmabostian/developer-portfolios); [bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com)
- **Weakness, template sameness**: Brittany's v4 has 4.2k forks despite her note that it was "not built as a starter theme". Cloned layouts dilute differentiation. — [GitHub bchiang7/v4](https://github.com/bchiang7/v4)

### Inferences
- Top-tier formula: positioning line, then 3-6 named proofs with outcomes, then writing or thinking, then personality, then easy contact. Visual flair is optional. A clear point of view is not.
- Almost none of the admired sites use long case studies with problem, approach and impact. Their proof is external adoption. Engineers without famous OSS should therefore add short case studies with metrics to make up for it.
- Making a site dense and personal (Fu, Paco) reads as more senior than heavy animation, unless the role is creative development.

### Gaps
- No quantitative hiring-manager survey data was found on what recruiters value in portfolio sites.
- Lighthouse/performance and mobile were not measured for the catalogued sites.

## Which ones are full-stack/backend engineers, and how do they showcase non-visual work?

### Takeaway
Backend and systems engineers in the top tier almost never use visual showpieces. They show non-visual work through (a) interactive explainers of systems concepts (Sam Rose), (b) deep, categorized technical writing and zines (Julia Evans), (c) prolific build logs and OSS tools with real users (Simon Willison: Datasette, sqlite-utils), and (d) organized OSS or infrastructure ownership lists (Anthony Fu: tooling; Lee Robinson: ML and infra writing). None of the visited sites shows architecture diagrams or scale metrics in a classic case-study format.

### Cited Findings
- Sam Rose turns backend concepts (load balancing, hashing, memory allocation, Bloom filters, queueing, retries, Kubernetes probes, prompt caching) into interactive visual essays. Companies (Encore, ngrok) commissioned these. — [samwho.dev](https://samwho.dev)
- Julia Evans covers DNS, TCP/networking, Linux tracing (strace, perf, eBPF), containers, SQLite internals and Git internals, plus zines. Posts as recent as Sep 2026 (SQLite operations, Django). — [jvns.ca](https://jvns.ca)
- Simon Willison: Datasette is the most-tagged topic (about 1,557 posts), plus LLM CLI and sqlite-utils. TILs and release notes are a running public log of backend and data-tooling work. — [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net)
- Anthony Fu presents infrastructure and tooling (Vitest, Vite DevTools, Shiki, ESLint Stylistic) as a role-grouped list linking to GitHub repos. Repos and adoption stand in for screenshots. — [antfu.me](https://antfu.me)
- Lee Robinson's site lists topics on AI model training, low-level software built with coding agents, and Rust, in plain text only. — [leerob.com](https://leerob.com)
- Delba presents docs and education systems (information architecture, user personas, diagram systems) as non-visual product work with stated ownership. — [delba.dev](https://delba.dev)
- Dan Abramov's recent posts (RSC Explorer, atproto "Social Filesystem") show protocol and architecture thinking through long-form essays and tools. — [overreacted.io](https://overreacted.io)
- HN-community sites (hnpwd directory; Jeff Geerling) are mostly blog-first, consistent with this pattern. — [HNPWD](https://hnpwd.github.io)

### Inferences
- For a full-stack or backend engineer, the strongest replicable patterns are: a few interactive or diagrammed explainers of systems they actually built (samwho-style), short case studies with scale metrics (requests/sec, latency, cost, data volume), and a role-grouped "built / maintain / contributed" list linking to code (antfu-style).
- Writing frequency and depth signal systems seniority better than UI polish. A lightweight TIL or notes feed (Willison, Lee's Notes) is a low-cost way to show ongoing work.

### Gaps
- No visited site showed a classic backend case study (architecture diagram plus metrics). Examples of that format were not found, so whether it works better can't be confirmed.
- Kent C. Dodds, Chris Coyier, Dan Luu, Jeff Geerling's site and other backend-heavy personal sites were not visited because of the tool-call budget.
