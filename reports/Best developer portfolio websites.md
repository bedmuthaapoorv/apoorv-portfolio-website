# Build proof, not polish, to win

The best developer portfolios of 2024-2026 are not the flashiest. They open with one sharp positioning line and back it with **named, verifiable proof**: shipped libraries, quantified outcomes, or a deep and steady stream of writing. Visual spectacle is optional and often costs accessibility. For a Full Stack SDE2 in India, the evidence points to one opening the market leaves almost entirely empty. Hiring managers want to see what *you* owned at work, how far your scope has grown, and the measured impact. But most SDE2 code lives in private repos, and none of the admired sites (global or Indian) publishes classic production case studies with architecture diagrams and before/after metrics. Recruiters spend seconds on a first pass and screen mainly on resume, LinkedIn and GitHub. So the portfolio works best as a **verification hub** for people who are already interested: shortlisting managers, interviewers preparing for your loop, and remote employers who cannot watch you work. Win by combining four things: a hero that does the 7-second resume scan's job, 2-3 deep case studies of work systems you owned, a lightweight dated notes feed, and a site whose own engineering (speed, accessibility, tests, CI, a public colophon) is the full-stack proof. Avoid template tells, AI chatbots as the main interface, and anything that hides content behind animation.

## Admired sites win on external proof, not visual effects

The most-cited portfolios fall into three groups, and two of them barely depend on design. The first is the "resume as website" template, defined by **Brittany Chiang**: a sticky left column with name, tagline and anchor nav, a scrolling timeline of six roles with linked companies and tech tags, project cards with outcome stats like "100k+ Installs", a résumé PDF, and a footer crediting her stack (Next.js, Tailwind, Vercel) ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)). Her previous v4 has **8.3k stars and 4.2k forks**, even though the README says it "was not built as a starter theme". That is why so many junior portfolios look the same ([GitHub bchiang7/v4](https://github.com/bchiang7/v4)). The second group is craft-led design engineers. Paco Coursey, Rauno Freiberg and Emil Kowalski have all moved toward minimalism, and their real credential is open-source code used across the industry: cmdk and next-themes, Sonner and Vaul ([paco.me](https://paco.me); [emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski)). Bruno Simon's driveable 3D car world, built with Three.js, Rapier physics and Howler, is the standard "portfolio as game", but it belongs to a creative developer who sells a Three.js course ([bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com)). The third group puts writing first, and the blog is the portfolio: Josh Comeau's interactive tutorials and courses, Lee Robinson's plain text-and-links notes, Simon Willison's near-daily posts since 2002, Julia Evans's 60+ categories of systems explainers ([joshwcomeau.com](https://www.joshwcomeau.com); [leerob.com](https://leerob.com); [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net); [jvns.ca](https://jvns.ca)).

Across all three groups the formula repeats: **a positioning line, then named proofs with outcomes, then writing or thinking, then personality, then easy contact**. Delba Oliveira describes work with ownership verbs, saying she "built, authored, maintained" the Next.js Docs, and states who used them ([delba.dev](https://delba.dev)). Anthony Fu lists projects as plain text grouped by role: Creator of Vitest, VueUse and UnoCSS; core team of Vue, Nuxt and Vite; maintainer of Shiki ([antfu.me](https://antfu.me)). Personality shows up in small, cheap details: Brittany's TARDIS easter egg, Josh's sound toggle, Rauno's manifesto, Paco's "Now" section with playlists.

Full-stack and backend engineers in the top tier almost never use visual showpieces. They make non-visual work visible in other ways. Sam Rose turns load balancing, hashing, Bloom filters, queueing and retries into **interactive visual essays**, and companies like Encore and ngrok have paid him to write them ([samwho.dev](https://samwho.dev)). Simon Willison's TILs and release notes form a running public log of data-tooling work, with Datasette tagged in roughly 1,557 posts ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net)). Dan Abramov used his blog for a job search with a post titled "Hire Me in Japan" ([overreacted.io](https://overreacted.io)).

The leaders have clear weaknesses you can exploit. Spectacle costs fundamentals: Awwwards' Developer Award gave Henri Heymans **8.00 for animations but 6.60 for accessibility and markup** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/henri-heymans-25)). Staleness is common. Rauno, Paco, Emil and samwho show no dates on their homepages, and Rauno's newest version archive is from 2023 ([rauno.me](https://rauno.me)). Cassie Evans replaced her famous animated site with a farewell note ([cassie.codes](https://www.cassie.codes)). And none of these sites shows a case study in the problem, architecture, trade-offs, metrics format. Their proof is outside adoption, which an SDE2 without a famous library does not have.

## Indian peers leave production case studies unclaimed

The Indian sites that get noticed belong to people with audiences, and they win on **volume and specificity of writing**, not design. Arpit Bhayani (Principal Engineer II at Razorpay, formerly Google and Amazon) has about 112 blogs, **about 673 short notes**, 268 videos, an annotated paper shelf and DiceDB (~10.8k stars) ([arpitbhayani.me](https://arpitbhayani.me)). Akash Rajpurohit (Staff Engineer, Levels.fyi) builds his brand on concrete numbers like "**$28,900/year saved** on Levels.fyi's AWS bill" and four apps running for about $45 a month, and those posts feed a fixed-price consulting offer ([akashrajpurohit.com](https://akashrajpurohit.com)). Ajit Singh (Agoda) frames 292 posts around specific problems such as "zombie leaders" and write hotspots, and adds interactive tools like cron generators and Snowflake/ULID decoders that give visitors a reason to come back ([singhajit.com](https://singhajit.com)). Sourav Dey adds a new 2026 pattern: "Ask ChatGPT/Claude about me" links with pre-filled prompts, aimed at AI discoverability ([souravdey.space](https://souravdey.space)).

The weaknesses are just as instructive. Sourav claims "scalable products serving millions" with no case study behind it. Anurag Hazra, creator of github-readme-stats, has a placeholder `#` resume link, empty alt text and stale Heroku demos ([anuraghazra.dev](https://anuraghazra.dev)). Piyush Garg's stats get stuck on "loading", Hitesh Choudhary's counters show "0", and Kunal Kushwaha's footer still says 2024 ([piyushgarg.dev](https://www.piyushgarg.dev); [hiteshchoudhary.com](https://hiteshchoudhary.com); [techwithkunal.com](https://techwithkunal.com/)). **Decay is the norm.** A maintained, dated, unbroken site already beats much of the field.

The typical rank-and-file Indian portfolio follows a fresher template: Hero, About, Skills, Projects, Contact. It is often hosted on vercel.app, github.io or is-a.dev subdomains, and a Hyderabad developer's guide lists the usual failures as no SEO, `#blog` anchors instead of real post URLs, and missing live links ([pavankiran.is-a.dev](https://pavankiran.is-a.dev/blog/portfolio-website-that-gets-hired)). Templates and inspiration come from a small shared pool: Aceternity UI (about 3M page views a month) is made by Manu Arora, whose own site credits another designer's site as its inspiration ([manuarora.in](https://manuarora.in)). The research found **no SDE-level Indian portfolio publishing production deep-dives** on Indian-scale problems (sale-day peaks, UPI retries, idempotency, reconciliation). That gap is based on a small sample, not a census, but it matches the global picture and is the clearest opening available.

## Recruiters skim in seconds; hiring managers read for ownership

Be honest about what a portfolio can do. The best survey available, Profy.dev's poll of 60+ hiring managers (**2021, now dated**), found most would look at a candidate's site, but most did not think lacking one hurts. Secondary summaries put it at 93% likely to look and 51% saying no site means equal chances ([DEV / Profy.dev](https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge); [Profy via HN reader](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://profy.dev/article/portfolio-websites-survey)). Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study measured a **7.4-second** first resume screen focused on titles and subheads, though its method has been criticised ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/); [ERE](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)). One engineering guide says frontend recruiters often click through to personal sites while backend recruiters rarely do, which helps a full-stack engineer whose site is itself frontend evidence ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)). On HN, experiences ranged from "past pretty much every first round" to "never got me anything" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41656015)). No 2023-2026 primary data exists, and none from Naukri, Instahyre, Cutshort or Wellfound.

What hiring managers do value is clear. Swizec Teller says a mid-career candidate must answer "**What's your slope?**" (growth in scope over time) and "What did you do?", and that side projects "shouldn't look more impressive than their paid work" ([swizec.com](https://swizec.com/blog/what-makes-your-resume-stand-out/)). Hiring managers look for evidence of architectural ownership and decisions over time, and treat "participated in code reviews" as table stakes ([The Architect's Notebook](https://thearchitectsnotebook.substack.com/p/ep-66-the-software-architect-resume)). One manager said a candid write-up of a *failed* project raised his team's confidence in the candidate. Hired's then-SVP of People said a clear statement of current role is the first thing managers check ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/can-a-personal-website-help-your-job-search-what-6-hiring-managers-really-think)). A weak site can also hurt you. One hiring commenter rejected candidates whose sites were "overengineered, using inappropriate technology for such a simple site", and another noted that "websites have to be maintained" ([DEV / Profy.dev](https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge)).

India adds its own wrinkles. Sourcing runs through LinkedIn, Naukri, referrals and a large consultant layer ([Saurabh Saha](https://saurabhsaha.substack.com/p/the-tech-recruitment-conundrum)). HR often uses LeetCode counts as a proxy; one Google India recruiter asked an SDE2 candidate for 200-300 medium/hard problems ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/google-recruiter-asked-for-leetcode-profile-and-told-me-to-complete-200-300-mid-hard-level-problems-mqeyhdry)). Demand has shifted toward GCCs, which rose from about 15% to **27% of IT demand** in 2025, and toward mid-level professionals ([Free Press Journal / Quess](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/indias-it-job-demand-touches-18-million-in-2025-led-by-gccs-product-firms)). For domestic roles the portfolio supports a LinkedIn and referral funnel, so the hero, LinkedIn headline and resume should use the same keywords. **Remote and international roles reward the portfolio most.** Anecdotal reports say US firms rarely hire directly from India unless the candidate is publicly visible ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/us-startups-offering-remote-role-in-india-yupwsqgs)). When an employer cannot watch you work, written proof of ownership and communication stands in for trust. Over the long run, niche writing on your own domain compounds: HN posters report years of inbound consulting and job offers from specialist posts ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41656015)), and Arpit Bhayani recommends hosting technical writing on a domain you own ([arpitbhayani.me](https://arpitbhayani.me/videos/software-engineers-guide-to-technical-writing)).

## Restraint, typography and accessibility beat template effects

In 2026, polish no longer stands out. DesignRush's view is that "**polished is the new generic**": when anyone can produce a smooth landing page, purple-to-magenta gradients and AI imagery read as filler, and typography becomes the main carrier of personality ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/trends/web-design-trends)). A May 2026 retrospective found bento grids and dark mode "keep winning", while 3D/WebGL, glassmorphism and kinetic type "underdelivered" ([StudioMeyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)). Bento works only "when the sizes mean something" ([line25](https://line25.com/articles/web-design-trends-2026/)). The particle-field hero with a rotating logo is explicitly called over-saturated ([Svilenkovic](https://www.svilenkovic.com/3d/3d-portfolio-trends-2026)). A developer who built a terminal-style portfolio found recruiters felt it "demanded too much participation" and rebuilt it as a conventional UI with the personality kept in smaller touches ([DEV](https://dev.to/zenoguy/why-i-ditched-terminal-uis-for-recruiters-57p7)).

Template fatigue has two sources. One is the many clones of Chiang's navy-and-teal sidebar layout ([CSSNectar](https://cssnectar.com/?p=21025)). The other is the shadcn animated-component registries: Aceternity ships 262 components and is called "the de facto reference for premium-feeling React landing pages in 2026", and Magic UI gives away a portfolio template ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/aceternity-ui-vs-magic-ui-vs-shadcn-animated-react-2026)). The visual tells are spotlight or aurora hero backgrounds, orbiting tech-stack icons, marquee logo strips, 3D tilt cards, stock zinc-plus-Geist styling, and skill progress bars. Any one of these is fine. Several stacked together signals "assembled, not designed". Using Radix/shadcn *primitives* for accessible behaviour does not cause this problem.

The web platform now handles motion natively. Same-document View Transitions reached Baseline in October 2025 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available?hl=en)), and CSS scroll-driven animations reached Baseline in **October 2026** with Firefox 159 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026)). Smooth page transitions and scroll "chapters" for case studies therefore need no JS library and degrade gracefully. Framer Motion is now Motion (`motion/react`, MIT) ([Motion](https://motion.dev/blog/framer-motion-is-now-independent-introducing-motion)). GSAP is fully free, but its licence is restrictive and not open source ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [Motion](https://motion.dev/docs/gsap-vs-motion)).

Accessibility is where flashy sites fail and where you can show seniority. WCAG 2.2 AA added **Focus Not Obscured** (focus must not hide behind a sticky nav), **24×24 px minimum targets**, and single-pointer alternatives to dragging ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Parallax, scroll reveals, page transitions and smooth scroll should be reduced under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Autoplaying or looping content needs pause controls ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/prefers-reduced-motion-accessible-animation/)). Reviewers often browse on phones ([Svilenkovic](https://www.svilenkovic.com/3d/3d-portfolio-trends-2026)), so any intro loader or WebGL gate in front of the hero works against you.

On features, the strongest-supported differentiator is **proof of work you can touch**. In a study of 16 design leaders, the average portfolio decision took 55 seconds of "clicking around" ([UX Tools](https://www.uxtools.co/blog/the-portfolio-is-becoming-a-playground)). That is design hiring, but the lesson carries over. A /now page is an established IndieWeb convention with 2,300+ listed adopters and costs almost nothing ([sive.rs](https://sive.rs/now2)). AI "ask my resume" chatbots are everywhere by 2025-26. Builders have had to add guardrails against prompts that inflate titles, and no recruiter-side evidence of value exists ([DEV](https://dev.to/adebayoomolumo/i-built-an-ai-portfolio-that-actually-answers-your-questions-43d5)). No source measured recruiter reactions to command palettes, guestbooks, Spotify widgets or visitor counters, so treat all of them as personality rather than hiring signal.

## Next.js 16 or Astro 6, held to a public performance budget

The 2026 consensus is **Astro for content-first sites and Next.js for app-like full-stack showcases** ([Cosmic](https://www.cosmicjs.com/blog/astro-vs-nextjs-2026)). Astro ships zero JS by default, and one blog benchmark puts Next.js at about 85-120KB on a blog page ([pkgpulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/astro-5-vs-nextjs-15-content-sites-2026)). Astro's team joined Cloudflare in January 2026 under a pledge to stay MIT-licensed and cloud-agnostic ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/astro-joins-cloudflare/)). Next.js 16 made Turbopack the default and introduced explicit Cache Components (`"use cache"`), which become the default in Next.js 17 ([Next.js 16](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16); [Next.js 16.4](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16-4)). Avoid Remix 3: it is an unstable non-React rewrite ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/08/remix-run-v3-drops-react)). This repo's history (a `sharp` image-optimisation commit, mobile-view issues) points to an existing Next.js codebase, and React/Next.js matches Indian full-stack job descriptions, so **Next.js 16 App Router** is the pragmatic choice. Server Components keep client JS small, and Server Actions and route handlers let the site demonstrate backend work. Pair it with Tailwind v4 (CSS-first `@theme`), shadcn primitives with a custom visual skin, and `tw-animate-css` in place of the deprecated `tailwindcss-animate` ([skills.sh](https://skills.sh/jezweb/claude-skills/tailwind-v4-shadcn)). For content, use in-repo MDX via Content Collections or Velite. **Do not use Contentlayer**, which has been unmaintained since April 2023 ([wisp.blog](https://www.wisp.blog/blog/contentlayer-has-been-abandoned-what-are-the-alternatives)). Generate per-page OG images with `opengraph-image.tsx` and `next/og` ([MakerKit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/md/tutorials/dynamic-og-image)).

Hold the site to the official Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile: **LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1**, on mobile and desktop alike ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)). Ignore blog claims that 2025 tightened these thresholds. Render hero text on the server, load the hero image as AVIF/WebP with priority, self-host subset variable fonts, and lazy-load any 3D or heavy animation after LCP behind reduced-motion checks. Enforce a Lighthouse CI budget in GitHub Actions, and publish real RUM numbers on a /colophon page so visitors can see you keep your performance claims.

For SEO, ranking for your own name comes down to the basics most peers skip. Put your name in the domain, title and H1. Add JSON-LD `ProfilePage` with `mainEntity: Person` and `sameAs` links to GitHub and LinkedIn ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)), `BlogPosting` with an author reference on posts, a real URL per post, a sitemap, RSS and canonicals. llms.txt is cheap but does not move rankings. Ahrefs found **97% of llms.txt files got zero AI requests** in May 2026, and Google says it neither helps nor hurts ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/); [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com/blog/llms-txt-search-visibility/)). Add it as an agent-readiness demo, not a growth tactic.

For hosting, Vercel Hobby is free for non-commercial personal use. Commercial use, such as a paid consulting page, needs Pro at $20 per seat per month ([Vercel docs](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)). Vercel has a Mumbai region (`bom1`, AWS ap-south-1) for functions that sit near an Indian database ([Vercel regions](https://vercel.com/docs/regions)). Cloudflare's free tier allows commercial use and unlimited bandwidth ([hostfleet](https://hostfleet.pages.dev/cf-pages-vs-vercel-vs-netlify-2026/)). Back-end features can run on free tiers: Upstash Redis (~500K commands a month) for rate limits and counters, Turso (5GB, 500M row reads) for relational data ([agentdeals Upstash](https://agentdeals.dev/vendor/upstash); [agentdeals Turso](https://agentdeals.dev/vendor/turso)). Note that Lee Robinson cut his own site back to an optional Postgres redirects table, dropping the guestbook and view counters ([github.com/leerob/site](https://github.com/leerob/site)). Choose two or three well-built back-end features rather than a pile of gimmicks.

For analytics, cookieless tools avoid a consent banner: Vercel Analytics is free on Vercel, and Umami Cloud is free up to 100k events ([pkgpulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/vercel-analytics-vs-plausible-vs-umami-privacy-first-2026); [aicores](https://aicores.io/pricing/umami)). India's DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025, and the core consent and notice duties phase in about 18 months later ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified); [myndsolution](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/rules/)). Contact-form and chatbot data counts as personal data, so publish a short privacy note with a grievance contact ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/impact-of-the-new-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-on-websites)). Whether a purely personal site falls under the Act is unresolved.

## The blueprint: sections, features and differentiators

The table below turns the evidence into a build plan. Order matters. Content that serves the 7-second skim comes first. Depth for interviewers and remote employers comes next, then personality.

| Section / page | What it contains | Why it beats the field |
|---|---|---|
| Hero (no loader, server-rendered) | Name; "Full Stack Engineer (SDE2), N yrs, Company"; one-line specialty; one headline metric; Resume PDF, email, LinkedIn, GitHub; "Based in [city], IST; open to remote with US/EU overlap" | Does the resume skim's job; current role is what managers check first ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/can-a-personal-website-help-your-job-search-what-6-hiring-managers-really-think)) |
| Experience as slope | Roles as a timeline showing scope growth (feature, then module, then migration lead), 2-3 quantified bullets each, ownership verbs | Answers "What's your slope?" ([swizec.com](https://swizec.com/blog/what-makes-your-resume-stand-out/)) |
| 2-3 production case studies | Context, constraints, your role, architecture diagram, decisions and trade-offs, before/after metrics (latency, throughput, cost, error rate), one thing you'd redo; one scroll-driven "chapter" each; sanitised for confidentiality | The format no admired global or Indian site uses; makes private-repo work visible |
| One interactive systems explainer | A samwho-style widget for something you built (e.g., an idempotency or retry visualiser, a claims-flow state machine) | Touchable proof for backend work ([samwho.dev](https://samwho.dev)) |
| Built / maintain / contributed | Antfu-style role-grouped list of side projects and OSS, kept short and below paid work | Side projects must not outshine paid work |
| Notes + writing (dated, RSS) | Weekly short notes plus occasional long posts in your domain; real URLs; "last updated" stamps | Arpit's notes model at small scale; dates fix the top sites' staleness problem |
| /now, /uses, /colophon | Current focus; tools; live CWV numbers, architecture diagram, ADRs, link to public repo with CI | Cheap authenticity, and the site's own engineering becomes full-stack proof |
| Contact + footer | Rate-limited Server Action form (Zod, Upstash, Turnstile), email copy button, resume again, privacy note | Contact appears in both hero and footer; DPDP-ready |
| Optional /work-with-me | Mentoring, talks, consulting, kept separate from the job path | Keeps the hiring path uncluttered ([arpitbhayani.me/consult](https://arpitbhayani.me/consult)) |

Signature differentiators, in priority order. First, the case studies themselves. If your domain is health insurance or claims processing, writing on claims pipelines, document ingestion and DPDP compliance is a niche the research found no Indian peer covering (an inference from absence). Second, visible upkeep: dates, no broken widgets, a current footer. Third, a distinctive typographic identity instead of a stock component look. Fourth, one well-made signature interaction (a native View Transition between case studies, or a keyboard-accessible ⌘K palette alongside normal nav) instead of many effects. Fifth, a public repo with strict TypeScript, Vitest and Playwright tests, a Lighthouse budget gate and preview deploys. Leave out skill bars, generic "Services", LeetCode widgets unless your rating is exceptional, follower-count "Reach" blocks, published CTC, and any AI chatbot as the main interface. If you want an AI touch, do what Sourav Dey does: link to pre-filled "ask an AI about me" prompts, and make sure your consistent entity data (the same name, bio and `sameAs` everywhere) gives the AI something accurate to find. Make the personal domain the canonical home, then cross-post to LinkedIn, Hashnode, Dev.to and Peerlist with links back ([Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/)).

## Conclusion

The real competition is not Bruno Simon's 3D world or Brittany Chiang's layout. It is the empty space between them: famous sites prove skill through outside adoption you cannot copy, and typical Indian SDE portfolios prove almost nothing beyond a skills list. A mid-level engineer's strongest material, owned production systems with measured outcomes, almost never appears on any portfolio because it sits behind NDAs and private repos. Writing it up carefully, with diagrams and numbers, turns the site from a resume duplicate into something only you can publish. That is what makes a portfolio "better" for hiring rather than for awards.

Two caveats should shape effort. The evidence that recruiters visit portfolios is thin and dated, so the site's return comes mostly from interview prep by managers who have already shortlisted you, from remote employers, and from writing that compounds over years. That argues for spending more on case studies and a sustainable notes habit than on visual engineering. Second, the site will be judged as a product you maintain. A modest site that is fast, accessible, dated and unbroken, with its own CI and RUM numbers on show, signals more seniority than an ambitious one that decays like most of the field.
